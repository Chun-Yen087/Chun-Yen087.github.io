About

I build the reporting that business decisions run on.

I'm a Computer Science co-op student at York University, currently a Wealth Analytics Intern at Scotiabank. My work sits between raw data and the people who act on it.

Program

BA, Computer Science (Co-op), York University. Expected graduation: August 2027.

Before York, I studied Electronics Engineering at Seneca College and Electrical Engineering at National Taipei University of Technology.

What I value in my program

Computer Science taught me to follow data the whole way: how it is stored, how it moves through a pipeline, and how it reaches the screen where someone makes a decision.

My security coursework added a second habit. I check whether numbers are accurate, traceable and handled with care. In banking, that habit matters as much as the code.

Most rewarding co-op

Scotiabank, Canadian Wealth Management Analytics. I refresh the Total Wealth Analyzer, a Power BI dashboard used by wealth leadership, regional directors, branch managers and advisors.

I am also a first point of contact for its users. Questions about missing data or plans that had not updated showed me that a dashboard is judged by the people who use it, not by its data model.

To see those requests clearly, I built an intake tracker that shows support volume by month. It was the point where I moved from maintaining a tool to improving it.

Career goal

To build a career in data and analytics within financial services, especially wealth management and pension fund investment teams, creating reporting and pipelines that help leaders decide with confidence.

Longer term, I want to use AI agents to solve real-world problems. Much of analytics work is repetitive: pulling data, checking it and answering the same questions. Agents can take on those steps and make teams faster and more efficient, so people can spend their time on the decisions themselves. I have started on this path by building an LLM workflow into my market dashboard project.
