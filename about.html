What I learned at York

York gave me a solid foundation in computer science: programming, databases, algorithms and software design.

It was my co-op at Toronto Hydro where I found my real passion. Turning thousands of operational records into dashboards that managers actually used showed me that I want to work with data, and that has been my direction ever since.

Rewarding co-op experience

Scotiabank, Canadian Wealth Management Analytics. Wealth management runs on trust and timing. Advisors and leaders across ScotiaMcLeod, Private Banking and Scotiatrust need accurate data on KYC, referrals and financial planning to serve clients well, and that data has to line up across every business line.

I feel lucky to be on a team whose work feeds directly into business decisions. Branch managers and regional directors use the Total Wealth Analyzer to see where their teams stand and what to act on next. Supporting those users taught me that a dashboard is only useful when people trust it and understand it.

Toronto Hydro, Operations & Data Analytics. I saw the same lesson in a different industry. Engineering, finance and operations teams relied on work-order and capital-planning data to schedule work and manage contractors. Cleaning 40,000+ records and building Tableau dashboards and KPI scorecards showed me how reliable data gives teams clearer priorities and faster decisions.

Career goal

To build a career in data and analytics within financial services, especially wealth management and pension fund investment teams, creating reporting and pipelines that help leaders decide with confidence.

Longer term, I want to use AI agents to solve real-world problems. Much of analytics work is repetitive: pulling data, checking it and answering the same questions. Agents can take on those steps and make teams faster and more efficient, so people can spend their time on the decisions themselves. I have started on this path by building an LLM workflow into my market dashboard project.
