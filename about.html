About

Hi, I'm ChunYen. I started in hardware, moved into code, and found my place in data.

I'm a Computer Science co-op student at York University. Right now I'm a Wealth Analytics Intern at Scotiabank, where I help keep a Power BI dashboard running for wealth management teams across Canada.

How I got here

My first studies were in hardware. I did Electrical Engineering at National Taipei University of Technology, then Electronics Engineering at Seneca College, where I programmed Siemens PLCs and worked with embedded systems and robotics.

Along the way I got interested in computer science and wanted to learn more, so I moved to York to study it. That's where I built my base in programming, databases and algorithms, and started taking data courses.

My first co-op, at Toronto Hydro, is where it clicked. A lot of my time went into cleaning operational data, more than 40,000 records pulled from SAP, SharePoint and Excel, and then building Tableau dashboards and contractor scorecards on top of it. Watching managers use that work in their monthly reporting made me want to keep doing it.

At Scotiabank, I refresh the Total Wealth Analyzer, a Power BI dashboard used by leaders, branch managers and advisors across ScotiaMcLeod, Private Banking and Scotiatrust. I pull Salesforce data with SOQL, process it with Alteryx, Python or SQL depending on the job, and run checks before anything goes out. I'm also the person users come to when a number on their dashboard looks off. I track down the cause, whether it's missing data, a plan that hasn't updated yet or a question about how a feature works, and get it sorted. That part of the job has taught me the most about how the data actually fits together.

Outside of work

A lot of my free time goes into investing, mostly tech companies. I follow semiconductors, AI infrastructure, cloud and enterprise software, read earnings reports, and try to work out which businesses actually have an edge and whether the price makes sense. I'd rather form my own view than take a headline at face value.

This started well before I worked at a bank, and it's a big part of why analytics and finance interest me. It's also how my market dashboard project began. I wanted a better way to make sense of all the numbers I was reading.

I also like building things just to see if they work. Most of my side projects start with a process that feels slower than it should be and the question of whether I can fix it. Away from the screen, I'm usually cooking and trying new recipes. Like my projects, the first version doesn't always work.

What I'm looking for

I want to keep building with data, whether that's in analytics, BI, data engineering or software. I've enjoyed working in financial services and would be glad to stay, but what matters most to me is working on problems where the data actually gets used.

I'm also curious about AI agents. A lot of analytics work repeats itself: pulling the same data, running the same checks, answering the same questions. I'd like to build tools that take on those steps. I've started small. My market dashboard uses an LLM to write its summaries, and I built my scheduling app with an AI pair programmer.
